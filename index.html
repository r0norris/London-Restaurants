The Charter

Being the Articles by which the Table was chosen

❦ ❦ ❦

This map is for a particular evening: a party of middle-aged gentlemen, a serious piece of meat in a room with some history to it, a good table for five, and then a proper pint and a well-made cocktail, neither more than ten minutes' walk away. Every rule below serves that evening.

I. Of the Thirty

Thirty restaurants: 18 chop houses, steak houses and grills, and 12 traditional dining rooms. Meat is the point, hence the majority. But some of London's best nights out happen in rooms where the room is the point: Rules, Wiltons, the Ritz, the Grand Divan at Simpson's. Traditional means carving trolleys, cuts and classics, not twelve-course tasting menus. Thirty is enough for a choice in every part of town without padding the list.

II. Of the Reckoning

Each restaurant is scored out of ten on four counts, then weighted:

40%Food. It is a dinner, so the cooking outweighs everything else, but cooking alone does not win. Informed by the Michelin Guide, the World's 101 Best Steak Restaurants 2026, Time Out and the Good Food Guide. 25%Character. Heritage, the room, a sense of occasion. If a group crosses London for dinner, it should feel like somewhere. 20%Group suitability. How easily a party of five gets a good table: online booking, how far ahead you must plan, deposits. A brilliant room booked solid for eight weeks is little use on a Thursday. That is why Brat ranks below its cooking, and Sweetings, beloved since 1889, sits last: lunch only and no bookings. 15%Meat. The tie-breaker for the brief. The great fish houses (Scott's, Sheekey, Bentley's) keep their places for the pescatarian minority, but pay for it here.

The sub-scores are editorial judgement, written down so they can be argued over. A total of 8.5 or more earns a gold star ★.

III. Of the Public House

A tavern makes the list only if it is really solid, meaning at least one of:

  • listed in CAMRA's current Good Beer Guide, where volunteers judge cask quality pint by pint;
  • on CAMRA's National Inventory of Historic Pub Interiors, because a Victorian interior cannot be faked;
  • consistently strong recent reviews for cask beer and atmosphere.

Most chosen taverns qualify on the first two. The gold star marks the standouts: three-star heritage interiors, Pub of the Year winners and fixtures of the Good Beer Guide. One honest exception: The Audley is the only tavern within reach of Scott's, and its heritage listing is regional, not national.

IV. Of the Cocktail Bar

Priority goes to the current World's 50 Best Bars and its 51–100 list (the Connaught, Tayēr + Elementary, Scarfes, Three Sheets). Then come the classic hotel bars, Dukes, the American Bar at the Savoy and the Rivoli, because a martini mixed at the table is the proper end to a chop-house night.

V. Of the Ten-Minute Walk

Every restaurant has at least one tavern and one cocktail bar within about 850 m in a straight line, roughly ten minutes on foot. A party that needs taxis between stops tends to become two parties. Neighbouring restaurants share their taverns.

VI. Of Truthfulness

  • No address, link or coordinate is invented. Anything that could not be confirmed is left blank and flagged with a dashed ⚠️ button.
  • Every venue was checked against its own website and one independent source (Hot Dinners' closures tracker, CAMRA WhatPub, Time Out, the Michelin Guide) and carries its evidence link.
  • Closed venues are struck off. A venue in doubt is shown greyed out with a warning.
  • Every link is machine-checked. The last run found none broken.

VIII. Of Prudence

Restaurants close and group policies change: minimum spends, set menus, deposits. Treat the group notes as a reason to telephone, not a promise, and re-check before booking.
